Cutthroat Atlas

Cutthroat Species Guide

A visual field-guide style overview of major cutthroat trout lineages, their historic watersheds, conservation questions, and the messy taxonomy behind western native trout.

Research Focus

Yellowfin Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii macdonaldi

Arkansas Basin Twin Lakes Presumed Extinct

Historically associated with Twin Lakes and the upper Arkansas Basin, Yellowfin cutthroat may have represented the native Arkansas Basin cutthroat lineage. One working idea is that large lake-form fish and smaller stream-resident fish were part of the same lineage, with juveniles or small adults possibly confused with early “greenback” records.

Greenback Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii stomias

South Platte Recovery Focus Taxonomy Debate

Greenback cutthroat are tied to the South Platte drainage, though historic records became complicated by early stocking, misidentification, and later genetic work. Their story overlaps heavily with the Yellowfin question because older sources often placed “greenbacks” in parts of the Arkansas Basin.

Colorado River Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii pleuriticus

Colorado River Basin Green Lineage West Slope

Colorado River cutthroat occupy the western-slope story of the Rockies. Green Lineage fish are especially interesting because some historical east-slope samples appear connected to this lineage, raising questions about ancient divide-crossing events, early stocking, or both.

San Juan Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii ssp. / San Juan lineage

San Juan Basin Rediscovered Lineage Wolf Creek Restoration

A native San Juan Basin cutthroat lineage once thought to be extinct, later confirmed through genetic comparison with preserved 1874 San Juan River specimens. Colorado Parks and Wildlife has worked to restore this lineage into protected headwater habitat, including Wolf Creek in the San Juan National Forest.

Green River Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii — Green River / Blue Lineage

Green River Basin Blue Lineage Colorado River Complex

Green River cutthroat represent the Blue Lineage within the Colorado River cutthroat complex. Their presence helps separate the old broad “Colorado River cutthroat” idea into multiple conservation lineages shaped by ancient drainage history and basin isolation.

Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii virginalis

Rio Grande Basin Southern Rockies Native Trout

Rio Grande cutthroat represent the southern Rocky Mountain cutthroat story, historically tied to the Rio Grande drainage and nearby headwaters. They are useful for comparing how different basins produced distinct native cutthroat lineages.

Lahontan Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi

Great Basin Lake Form Stream Form

Lahontan cutthroat are a great comparison point for Yellowfin because they show how one cutthroat lineage can include both large lake-dwelling forms and smaller stream-resident forms depending on habitat.

Bear River Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii — Bear River lineage

Bear River Bear Lake Form Yellowstone Affinity

Bear River cutthroat occupy the Bear River watershed in the Bonneville Basin, but genetic work suggests they are closer to Yellowstone/Snake River cutthroat than to Bonneville cutthroat from the rest of the basin. Bear Lake cutthroat represent the large lake-form expression of this lineage.

Bonneville Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii utah

Bonneville Basin Great Basin Ancient Lake System

Bonneville cutthroat are native to the ancient Lake Bonneville drainage, including parts of Utah, Idaho, Wyoming, and Nevada. Their modern story is tied to isolated streams, remnant pluvial lake basins, and long-running native trout restoration work.

Yellowstone Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii bouvieri

Yellowstone Basin Snake River Headwaters Interior West

Yellowstone cutthroat are one of the best-known interior cutthroat lineages, historically tied to the Yellowstone River, Snake River headwaters, and nearby high-elevation waters. They are central to the evolutionary story of several neighboring cutthroat forms.

Snake River Finespotted Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii behnkei

Snake River Fine Spotting Yellowstone Complex

Snake River finespotted cutthroat are closely associated with the Snake River drainage and are known for their dense, fine spotting pattern. They are often discussed alongside Yellowstone cutthroat because of their close geographic and evolutionary relationship.

Coastal Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii clarkii

Pacific Coast Sea-Run Forms Stream Resident Forms

Coastal cutthroat occupy the Pacific coastal range and can express both sea-run and stream-resident life histories. Their diversity makes them a useful comparison point for how one cutthroat lineage can adapt to very different habitats.

Westslope Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii lewisi

Northern Rockies Columbia Basin Missouri Headwaters

Westslope cutthroat are native across parts of the northern Rocky Mountains, including Columbia Basin waters and portions of the upper Missouri system. Their range helps frame the northern side of the cutthroat story.

Paiute Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii seleniris

Lahontan Basin Highly Restricted Recovery Lineage

Paiute cutthroat are a highly restricted Lahontan Basin lineage, historically associated with a small headwater system. Their conservation story is one of the clearest examples of a narrowly endemic cutthroat nearly lost and later restored through intensive recovery work.

Alvord Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii alvordensis

Alvord Basin Presumed Extinct Desert Basin

Alvord cutthroat were associated with the Alvord Basin of southeastern Oregon and northern Nevada. They are generally treated as extinct, making them one of the lost desert-basin branches of the broader cutthroat story.

Whitehorse Basin Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii — Whitehorse / Coyote Basin lineage

Whitehorse Basin Coyote Basin Desert Headwaters

Whitehorse Basin cutthroat are part of the desert-basin cutthroat complex of southeastern Oregon. Their isolated drainage history makes them useful for comparing how small interior basins produced distinct trout forms.

Humboldt Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii — Humboldt lineage

Humboldt River Lahontan Basin Interior Nevada

Humboldt cutthroat are tied to the Humboldt River system and the broader Lahontan Basin. Their pattern of isolated stream populations reflects the fragmented hydrology of Nevada’s interior basins.

Quinn River Cutthroat Trout

Oncorhynchus clarkii — Quinn River lineage

Quinn River Lahontan Basin Desert Stream Form

Quinn River cutthroat are a northeastern Lahontan Basin lineage associated with small desert-stream habitats. Their subtle coloration and restricted range make them another example of how isolated Great Basin waters shaped unique cutthroat forms.
