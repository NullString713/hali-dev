Data Philosophy

Cutthroat Atlas separates stream geometry, trout metadata, and watershed history into distinct layers. A stream or lake may appear in the public water network without automatically being treated as native trout habitat. Human-modified features such as canals, tunnels, reservoirs, and transbasin diversions will be handled separately from natural watershed layers when the data supports that distinction.

Data Status

This prototype uses public OpenStreetMap waterway geometry exported through Overpass. Trout history, species presence, and recovery status are currently prototype-level and will be refined with public agency, academic, museum, and conservation sources. The goal is to make public trout and watershed information easier to explore, compare, and question.

Data Sources

Cutthroat Atlas may combine public OpenStreetMap waterway geometry, USGS hydrography, public agency and conservation references, and clearly marked interpreted review layers. Source and confidence notes are kept separate so users can distinguish mapped geometry from trout history, restoration context, and areas that still need verification.

Planned Layers

Future layers may include USGS hydrography comparisons, interpreted watershed-review geometry, historic native range, current likely species, recovery and restoration status, public access context, and landowner-managed access opportunities where appropriate.
